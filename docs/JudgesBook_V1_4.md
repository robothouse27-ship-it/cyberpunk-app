This is the [RE]action System by Monster 
Fight Club®. This section will go over the 
basics of the system and its tools.
The [RE]action System is scenario-driven. 
These rules cover one-off games as 
well as playing a series of games in a 
campaign. In each scenario, the players 
will each control a team of miniatures on 
the table. 
[RE]ACTION SYSTEMTM
The [RE]action System uses three 
different colors of dice which link to three 
colors of actions that models can take. 
There are also colors of dice linked to 
other types of rolls, such as the Obstacle 
die.
ACTIONS
ACTIONS
When it’s your turn to play, you’ll be taking 
actions with your models. Actions include 
moving, attacking, healing, and scenario-
specific actions. 
Each model has a number of actions 
available to them, colored GREEN
GREEN, 
YELLOW
YELLOW, or RED
RED. You’ll use action 
tokens to track how many actions each 
Character has available.
When one of your models takes an action, 
they’ll flip the corresponding action token, 
to show it’s been used.
The color of the action token used 
determines what color die you’ll roll, 
to see if the action succeeds or fails. 
Generally, GREEN
GREEN actions are the best, 
then YELLOW
YELLOW, with RED 
 RED actions being 
least likely to succeed.
OPPOSED DICE ROLLS
OPPOSED DICE ROLLS
When you attempt an action, your 
opponent gets to make an Opposed Roll. 
If your roll is higher than theirs, the action 
succeeds. Otherwise, the action fails.
[RE]ACTIONS
[RE]ACTIONS
If one of your Characters is wounded by a 
rival’s attack, you’ll have the opportunity 
to react to it with an out-of-turn action.
THE DICE
Combat Zone primarily uses four different 
colors of dice. 
THE ACTION DICE
THE ACTION DICE
Three of the dice colors are called Action

action succeeds or not. These are the 
GREEN

THE OBSTACLE DIE
THE OBSTACLE DIE
The black ten-sided dice are called

Models are considered “the

a tie.
OTHER DICE
OTHER DICE
There are other rare types of dice used in 
the [RE]action system (such as a purple

these dice when needed.

CRIT & FUMBLE SYMBOLS
Each die also has a CRIT as its highest 
value and a FUMBLE in place of the 1.
Crits usually mean success, and can 
trigger special rules. 
Fumbles usually mean you have failed

trigger worse results.

There are three things you must do to prepare for a game in the [RE}action system: 
build your team, choose a scenario, and construct a Battlespace.
BUILDING A TEAM
Before the game, you’ll hire your team, consisting of a mixture of model types. You 
can also enhance your Characters by purchasing extra gear or other enhancements 
(such as programs in Cyberpunk RED: Combat Zone). 
SIZE OF GAMES
SIZE OF GAMES
Games in the [RE]action system can be played in about 45 minutes to an hour at 
competitive levels (100 to 150 EB in Cyberpunk RED: Combat Zone). Larger games 
(200+ points) are played on a larger table and take longer to play.
•	 100 points – 22” x 30”
•	 150 points – 22” x 30” 
•	 200 points – 44” x 30” 
CHOOSING A SCENARIO
Games in the [RE]action system are played using Scenarios. Each of the scenarios 
describes a different combat situation with its own goals, special rules, and setup. 
If you are playing a campaign, winning or losing may have consequences in 
future games.
WINNING A SCENARIO
WINNING A SCENARIO
Different scenarios have different goals, which might range from taking out rival 
models, stealing assets, capturing territory, or just about any other deed that needs 
doing. Each Scenario will define how a player scores points towards victory.
CONSTRUCTING A BATTLESPACE
The area your models will fight and move through is the Battlespace. Before you 
play, you must set up the Battlespace using scenery, guided by the instructions for 
the scenario. A miniatures game can be played on a variety of terrain tables and 
gaming boards.

BATTLESPACE DEFINITIONS
Scenarios use a variety of terms to indicate locations 
on the Battlespace. This is most common when 
defining team deployment areas or the placement of 
objectives and other scenario specific elements. This 
example assumes a 22” x 30” Battlespace/board.
BATTLESPACE / BOARD
BATTLESPACE / BOARD
The terms Battlespace and Board may be used 
interchangeably, and both refer to the playing 
surface. This area is typically flat, but some terrain 
tables may have elevation elements built in. The 
Battlespace definitions assume the surface is 
flat, and ignore any height when determining the 
following locations.
EDGES (LONG & SHORT)
EDGES (LONG & SHORT)
The four lines where the Battlespace ends are the 
board edges. The long board edges are the parallel 
edges longest in length (either 30”). The short board 
edges are the parallel lines shortest in length (either 
22”). If you’re playing on a non-standard board size, 
the players may need to define the board edges 
during setup.
CENTERLINE
CENTERLINE
There are two centerlines, one long and one short. 
The long centerline runs parallel to the long board 
edges exactly halfway between them (11”). The short 
centerline runs parallel to the short board edges 
exactly halfway between them (15”). 
CENTER
CENTER
The center of the Battlespace is the point at the 
middle of the board. The center is located at the 
point that is 11” from both long board edges and 15” 
from both short board edges. The center is marked 
with a green star in the above example.
QUARTER
QUARTER
There are four quarters. Each quarter is 11” x 15” 
and do not overlap. Each quarter also has a center 
(the center of each board quarter is marked with a 
red X in the above example. A quarter’s center is the 
point 5.5” along the quarter’s short edge and 7.5” 
along the quarter’s long edge.

TAKING ACTIONS
Action tokens are used to track a model’s actions. 
They show a Model’s number of actions, of what 
color, and whether those actions are ready or used. 
When a Character takes an action, choose one of its 
ready action tokens and flip it to its used side. This is 
important, since the color of the action token spent 
determines the color die roll when taking the action, 
or the distance the model moves.
STARTING ACTION TOKENS
STARTING ACTION TOKENS
Each Character begins the game with the action 
tokens shown on their card. Actions tokens begin the 
game ready. Place them on or near the card, so that 
it’s clear which tokens belong to which Character.
AVAILABLE ACTIONS
AVAILABLE ACTIONS
When a model is active, they can attempt actions. 
Characters may choose from a variety of actions, 
including basic actions (page 14), actions granted 
by cards, and scenario actions. Models may take 
the same action multiple times, (unless the action is 
Difficult). 
ATTACK ACTIONS
ATTACK ACTIONS
Attack actions may only target rival models and, if 
they are successful, they deal a wound to the target. 
Any action with the attack symbol (shown here) is 
an attack action. This icon may appear next to the 
attack’s text or attached to the attack’s skill icon.
FULLY RESOLVING ACTIONS
FULLY RESOLVING ACTIONS
Each action must be fully resolved before a model 
may start another action. If an event triggers another 
action (either on the acting model or another model) 
then finish resolving the current action before 
moving on to the triggered action.
FREE ACTIONS & MOVEMENT
FREE ACTIONS & MOVEMENT
When an ability or effect causes a model to take an 
action “for free” the model does not need to use an 
action token to take the action. To determine the 
color of the action, the model may choose any action 
token it has, ready or used (Gonks use the action 
color on their card). When an ability causes a model 
to move, this movement is also free (see page 21).
EASY & DIFFICULT ACTIONS
EASY & DIFFICULT ACTIONS
EASY
EASY actions don’t require a skill roll, they 
automatically succeed (an Action token is still used).
Normally, models choose any combination of 
available actions when they activate, including 
the same action multiple times. The exception is 
DIFFICULT
DIFFICULT actions. 
Difficult actions can be used once and can only be 
used again after you Inspire Your Team.
When you use a Difficult action, place a used action 
token on it as a reminder that it cannot be used. 
When you Inspire Your Team, remove the action 
token to show that it is available again.
EXHAUSTING CARDS
EXHAUSTING CARDS
When you are directed to “exhaust” a card you must 
flip the card over so it’s back is showing. When you 
refresh your team, you can also refresh (flip face-up) 
any exhausted cards.
GONK ACTIONS
GONK ACTIONS
Gonks take a single action whenever they’re 
activated and don’t use action tokens. 
Gonks may take any of the basic actions, except the 
basic Influence action (Command).
Since Gonks don’t use action tokens each Gonk 
model may take one action when you Inspire Your 
Team or when other models force them to act. 
They’ll always roll their action die (usually YELLOW
YELLOW) 
and if they suffer a wound, they’re taken out.

RESOLVING AN ACTION
After choosing an action and using an action token, 
you’ll do the following to resolve the action:
•	 CHOOSE THE ACTION’S TARGET:
CHOOSE THE ACTION’S TARGET: Make sure it’s 
within the action’s range and there is a Path of 
Attack (see page 24).
•	 DETERMINE OPPOSITION:
DETERMINE OPPOSITION: If the target is a rival, 
they choose which action token they will use for 
the Opposed roll. If the action does not target 
a rival model, the rival player will use the black 
Obstacle die for the Opposed roll.
•	 ROLL DICE:
ROLL DICE: Both players roll their dice at the same 
time and add the appropriate skill value, plus any 
other modifiers (such as cover, page 24).
•	 COMPARE RESULTS:
COMPARE RESULTS: If the acting Model’s total is 
higher, the action succeeds; otherwise it fails.
When a model attempts an action, you’ll roll a die to 
resolve whether the action succeeds or fails. You’ll 
roll the color die that matches the action token used 
to attempt the action. 
When an action targets a rival, the target rival 
chooses a die for their Opposed roll that matches 
any one of their action tokens; it don’t matter if 
they’re ready or used. The target doesn’t have to 
spend the token, but one of its tokens must be 
chosen to oppose the action.
If the action doesn’t target a rival model, the rival player 
rolls the black, ten-sided Obstacle die and does not add 
any skill to the roll. This means that actions without a 
target (like moving over an obstacle) or that target your 
own friendly models (like the basic Tech action) must 
roll against the Obstacle die to succeed
SUCCESS & FAILURE
SUCCESS & FAILURE
If the action is an attack, and it succeeds, then the

PLAYER TURNS
TAKING CONTROL
TAKING CONTROL
When you take control, you have to choose 
between Activating a Character or Inspiring  
Your Team. 
ACTIVATING A CHARACTER
ACTIVATING A CHARACTER
Choose a character with ready action tokens 
and take actions with them.
Characters can choose from any of the six 
basic actions (below) and may have more 
available, depending on their Character card 
and gear.
When your active Model is finished taking 
actions, control passes to your opponent.
MOVE: 
MOVE: Move up to your action color. 
If the move is unobstructed, it doesn’t 
require a Skill Roll.
MELEE ATTACK: 
MELEE ATTACK:  
Attempt to wound a rival within RED. 
Opposed by target’s Melee Skill.
RANGED ATTACK: 
RANGED ATTACK:  
Attempt to wound a rival within 
YELLOW 
YELLOW or GREEN 
 GREEN range. Opposed by 
target’s Reflexes.
BASIC MED:
BASIC MED: Attempt to Heal 1 from  
another Red-lined target within reach.
BASIC TECH: 
BASIC TECH:  
Remove 1 Hacked token from friendly 
target.
BASIC INFLUENCE: 
BASIC INFLUENCE:  
Activate Friendly Model.
INSPIRING YOUR TEAM
INSPIRING YOUR TEAM
Activate each of your Gonks by taking a single 
action with each of them. Then, refresh all your 
characters’ used action tokens.
USING THE LIMITER
USING THE LIMITER
Ranges in Combat Zone are measured with 
the Limiter. Most actions picture a color range 
bar and can only target models within that 
range. Models move the color range of the 
action token they used to move.

DETERMINE THE SIZE 
DETERMINE THE SIZE 
OF YOUR GAME 
OF YOUR GAME 
Agree with your opponent about 
how many EB each player is going to 
spend on their team. 100, 150, or 200 are 
good amounts, depending on how long 
you want to play.
BUILD YOUR TEAM
BUILD YOUR TEAM 
One Leader, multiple characters, 
plus some Gonks (limited by your 
character’s combined Influence).
CHOOSE YOUR SCENARIO
CHOOSE YOUR SCENARIO
Each scenario will contain details of 
how to set up the Battlespace and 
what you need to do to win.
PLAY THE GAME
PLAY THE GAME
At any one time, one player will 
have control and will take actions 
with their models. Control passes 
clockwise between players. Scenarios 
determine who has control first.
WINNING THE GAME
WINNING THE GAME
Play continues until someone wins 
by completing the goals of the 
scenario.
