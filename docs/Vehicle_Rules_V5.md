A vehicle is a special type of model that requires a driver. Vehicles don’t activate on 
their own. 
A vehicle might allow passengers to take special actions, but it does not activate or 
take actions on its own. Vehicle models are not characters. 
VEHICLE MODEL “BASE” 
Many vehicle models do not have a base, and instead the figure itself determines the 
area taken up on the Battlespace by the vehicle. Treat the figure’s top-down footprint 
as the base of the model. If a vehicle model has a base (such as a cycle), the model’s 
base is used as normal.
VEHICLE MODEL FACING
Unlike Characters and Gonks, vehicle models have a facing. The front of the vehicle 
model determines the front of the vehicle facing. When a vehicle moves, facing is 
important (see Piloting Vehicles on page 5). 
BUYING VEHICLES
A vehicle can be included in a team like any other model by paying EB from your 
team’s budget. Vehicles do not have a faction and can be taken by any team. 
VEHICLE MODS
VEHICLE MODS
Vehicles are not Characters, therefore they cannot equip normal Gear cards. Instead, 
vehicles have access to vehicle mods. Vehicle cards will denote a number of mod 
slots, each with a keyword (such as Steering). A vehicle mod may only be equipped 
in a mod slot that shares at least one keyword with it. A vehicle may only equip one 
vehicle mod per slot.
Vehicle mods are universal and may be purchased by any team. 
DEPLOYING VEHICLES
Vehicle models are placed during setup like any other model. In addition, other models 
may begin the game embarked in a vehicle (see Passengers on page 4). 
When the vehicle and other models are placed in reserve, declare which models are 
embarked in the vehicle and place them on its card.
When a vehicle arrives from reserve, if the scenario allows the model to deploy in 
contact with a board edge, the vehicle must be deployed in contact with a board edge. 
If the scenario doesn’t allow for reserves to deploy along the board edge, follow the 
scenario guidelines (this may lead to narratively odd situations).
VEHICLE INTERACTIONS
Vehicles may be attacked and targeted by other actions, with a few exceptions. 
•	 A vehicle model may not be the target of Influence actions.
•	 Vehicles may not be given free actions (including movement) by effects that do not 
specifically declare that they affect vehicle models. 
•	 Since a vehicle cannot be activated, it may not be targeted by Command effects, even 
those that aren’t Influence based.
Note that a vehicle is rarely the acting or active model. If a vehicle takes an action by 
itself (such as through a mod) or is forced to take an action by another model, then the 
vehicle is the acting model during that action.

ARMOR & HULL TOKENS (D)
ARMOR & HULL TOKENS (D)
Each card shows the vehicle’s armor value and how many Hull tokens 
the vehicle has to defend itself and of what color those tokens are at 
the start of the game. Hull tokens are a special kind of Action token 
that cannot be spent. Hull tokens are not used to take actions, they 
are only used by the vehicle to resist and track damage.
SPECIAL RULES (E)
SPECIAL RULES (E)
Many vehicles have a special rule or action listed on their card. This 
rule applies only to them, unless stated otherwise. If the special rule 
is an action, it will show a skill icon and state who can use the action 
(often the driver). Otherwise, it is an ability and does not require an 
action to use.
SEATS (F)
SEATS (F)
Vehicles have a seat for each passenger, which can be noted as a 
driver, gunner, or a seat number. The top of the card indicates the 
front of the vehicle, relative to the passenger seats.

MOD SLOTS (G)
Many vehicles have slots for specific types of mod slots, such as 
Engine or Systems. If a mod is shown next to a seat it is linked to 
that seat. Mods in a linked slot (like vehicle weapons) are only usable 
by models in the linked seat.

Every vehicle has a number of seats on its card to 
place models on, one for the driver and additional 
seats for passengers. A driver also counts as a 
passenger when the rules affect vehicle passengers. 
EMBARK:
EMBARK: When a Character or Gonk’s movement 
ends with the model within reach of any vehicle, the 
model may enter the vehicle. The model is removed 
from the board and placed on the vehicle card in an 
empty seat. If there are no empty seats, the model 
may not embark into the vehicle. A model may not 
embark during the same activation they disembark. 
DISEMBARK: 
DISEMBARK: When an embarked model takes a 
move action, they may exit the vehicle and be placed 
in base contact with the vehicle before moving. 
The model may then take its movement from the 
disembarked position. A model may not disembark 
during the same activation they embark.
EVERYONE OUT!: 
EVERYONE OUT!: At the end of a driver’s activation, 
all models embarked in the driver’s vehicle may 
choose to disembark (this is not an action). They do 
not move after deploying in this way.
SWITCH SEATS: 
SWITCH SEATS: A model in a vehicle may take an 
Easy Reflexes action to move to another empty seat 
in a vehicle they are a passenger in. 
GUNNERS
GUNNERS
A model in a gunner seat may fire weapons in the 
linked mod slot if the character begins its activation 
in that gunner seat.

PASSENGER ACTIONS
Passengers may take actions from within a vehicle. 
The Path of Attack is drawn from anywhere on the

A passenger may choose to use the vehicle’s armor

AERODYNES
From military VTOL to VIP commuters, aerodynes can be seen throughout the skies of 
Night City. In Cyberpunk RED: Combat Zone, we use these aerodyne rules to cover the 
vertical possibilities inherent in vehicles that operate at various altitudes and hover. 
ALTITUDES
ALTITUDES
There are two altitudes at which an aerodyne operates. Battle, & Overwatch. Each 
of these altitudes has its own special rules for governing the aerodyne’s movement, 
attacks, and other interactions. 
BATTLE ALTITUDE
The lowest altitude is Battle. When an aerodyne is at this altitude it operates like any 
other vehicle, except that it hovers above the ground. When an aerodyne’s pilot finishes 
a Steer action, it sets its hover height at RED
RED, YELLOW
YELLOW, or GREEN
GREEN. Place an Action 
token on the model’s base to signify its hover height. An aerodyne’s hover height may 
not be set lower than the height of terrain it is on (if the model is atop a YELLOW

BATTLE ALTITUDE ACTIONS

OVERWATCH ALTITUDE
At this altitude, the aerodyne is above the 
Battlespace. Due to its elevation, only effectively 
enclosed buildings, such as those with a roof or 
similar structure, impede attacks.
When an aerodyne is at this altitude place the 
model off the board in contact with a board edge. 
We keep the aerodyne near the Battlespace to 
maintain the model’s visual presence. 
The physical position isn’t important to gameplay, 
so feel free to move the vehicle to any edge. 
Passengers cannot disembark at this altitude.

OVERWATCH ALTITUDE ATTACKS
The vehicle is attacking from directly above the 
Battlespace, so only obstacles and barriers that 
provide cover from above will affect attacks 
from the vehicle. In addition, it is too far from the 
Battlespace for actions at any range other than 
Long to be affective.

OPPONENT BEFORE THE GAME. An enclosed area

However, the sheer advantage of such a high

CHANGING ALTITUDE 
An aerodyne’s pilot may elevate or lower the vehicle 
one step with a Steer action (they make no other 
movement).
When an aerodyne rises from Battle altitude to 
Overwatch altitude, set it off the board along a 
board edge.
When an aerodyne lowers from Overwatch altitude 
to Battle altitude, deploy it anywhere in the 
Battlespace.
When an aerodyne rises from Overwatch altitude to 
higher altitude, remove it from the Battlespace and 
set it in reserve.
